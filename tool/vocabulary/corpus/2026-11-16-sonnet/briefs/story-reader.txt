App: Dispatch: a daily news digest read as full-screen stories. Screens and features: Today (the day's eight stories as a stack of full-screen cards moved through by a vertical swipe, the arrows or the wheel, each settling into place: a drawn scene, a section label, a headline in large type and two lines of summary whose parts arrive one after another as the card comes in; a rail of eight marks at the side that shows where you are and jumps to a story; a card held under a finger or a pressed key pauses its motion), Story (a swipe up from a card's foot or Enter opens the full story as a sheet that slides over the card with a half stop for the summary and a full stop for the article: headline, byline, a reading time, the text with a pull quote and a figure, a chart with a table alternative, related stories; dragged down or Escape to close, giving back the card it came from), Reactions (a long press or a right click on a card opens a small row of five reactions that grows from the point pressed, one chosen with a bounce and counted on the card; save and share in the same menu), Sections (chips across the top that scroll and filter the stack, with an all-caught-up card at the end that shows tomorrow's time), Saved (a list with a row swiped to remove, the same in its menu, an empty state), Search (forgiving, matches marked, by section), Listen (a bar at the foot that reads a story: play and pause, a progress line that is scrubbed, speed), Settings (sections chosen and reordered by a handle, text size as a slider that changes the story at once, reduced motion switch that turns the arrivals off, theme). Read stories, saved stories, reactions and settings outlive a reload.

Style: editorial and vivid: each story's card in its section's deep colour with cream type, a strong condensed headline face over a calm reading serif, bold drawn scenes, with special focus on the swiped stack of stories settling one at a time, the story sheet with its two stops, and the reactions row from a long press.

Write every screen of the app, each under a line `### <screen name>`, starting with App (an app window) or Page (a page that scrolls). Overlays (dialogs, sheets, menus) go after the screen that opens them. Realistic content. Every interaction in the brief is written, not described. Output only the notation.
