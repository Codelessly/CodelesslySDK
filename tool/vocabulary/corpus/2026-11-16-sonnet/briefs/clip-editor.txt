App: Cutline: an audio editor for cutting a podcast episode. Screens and features: Timeline (three tracks: voice, guest, music; each a row with a name, mute and solo switches and a volume slider at its head; clips as blocks placed by their start and length along a ruler in minutes and seconds, each with a drawn waveform and a name; a clip dragged along its track and to another track, moved by the arrows when chosen, and snapping to the neighbours' edges and to the playhead; a clip's ends dragged to trim it, and by keyboard; split at the playhead; several clips chosen by Shift, Ctrl and a drag across, moved together; duplicate and delete from a clip's menu, delete asking first when several are chosen; a zoom slider and the wheel changing how much time the width shows; the timeline scrolls sideways and the track heads stay in place), Transport (play and pause with Space, a playhead that runs with the time and is dragged along the ruler, skip to the start and end, a time readout, a loop over a range marked on the ruler, speed choices), Clip panel (for the chosen clip: name, start and length as fields that step, gain slider, fade in and fade out sliders drawn on the clip as slopes, a colour), Library (clips listed with their length, searched, dragged onto a track), Markers (added at the playhead with a name, listed, pressed to jump there, shown on the ruler), Export (a dialog with format and quality choices, a progress bar that runs and can be cancelled, a done state), Undo and redo, a shortcuts list. On a phone one track is tall and the others are thin. The project outlives a reload.

Style: studio-dark and focused: charcoal ground, track colours in teal, coral and gold, a bright playhead, small monospace times, with special focus on clips dragged, trimmed and split along the timeline, the playhead and zoom, and the same work done by keyboard.

Write every screen of the app, each under a line `### <screen name>`, starting with App (an app window) or Page (a page that scrolls). Overlays (dialogs, sheets, menus) go after the screen that opens them. Realistic content. Every interaction in the brief is written, not described. Output only the notation.
